# Chris Spade — The Adult Child in the Middle (Researched Dossier)

## Credentials
- Real estate agent with **Keller Williams Realty**; probate specialist and senior
  relocation specialist.
- Host of ***Moving Mom and Downsizing Dad: a Gen Xer's Guide to Navigating Life and our
  Aging Parents*** — launched the week of **12 January 2025**, **30+ episodes** through
  August 2025.
- Came to the niche through his own family and friends' experience, not through a
  designation course — which is why the material is organised around what actually goes
  wrong rather than around a syllabus.

## Doctrine: talk to the son, not just the seller
He is the only seat at this table whose audience is explicitly **the adult child**, and
that is precisely why he earns a seat. On a senior listing the person who picks up the
phone, books the appointment, and drives the decision is very often a Gen X son or
daughter — and nobody in the existing 34 speaks to them directly.

His stated mission: give people the **"tools to make informed decisions and avoid crisis
mode."**

**"Crisis mode" is his organising idea.** The distinction that runs through the whole
show: a family that plans has options — price, timing, condition, tax treatment. A
family that waits for the fall, the diagnosis, or the death has none of them. Everything
he teaches is an argument for having the conversation early, while it is still boring.

## Coverage — what he has actually worked through on the show
The breadth is the point; the adult child's problem is never only the house.

- **Estate and legal:** probate avoidance, trusts vs wills, powers of attorney,
  incapacity planning, inheritance advances.
- **Financial:** Medicare enrollment and plan selection, tax consequences of a home
  sale, capital gains exclusion, 1031 exchanges and DSTs, reverse mortgages,
  long-term-care planning.
- **The house:** a **30-day market-ready checklist** that turns a lived-in family home
  into something buyers respond to; pre-listing sequencing; senior move managers; estate
  sales; aging-in-place modifications and accessibility work.
- **Care:** assisted living and memory care navigation, dementia and Lewy body
  specifics, loneliness and senior day programs, healthcare budgeting.
- **The human part:** caregiver burnout, family communication under strain, sentimental
  items, professional organisers.

## Real estate application
- The **first call is often to the child, not the owner** — and the child is exhausted,
  guilty, and working full time. Scripts in his voice respect that they have ten minutes.
- Sequence the money conversation before the listing conversation. Capital gains, the
  step-up, and reverse-mortgage payoff change what "we should sell" even means.
- **Never make the child the villain.** The agent's job is to be the neutral third party
  so the child doesn't have to be the one pushing their parent.
- The **30-day market-ready checklist** is the concrete deliverable — it converts an
  overwhelming house into a finite list, which is the single most calming thing an agent
  can hand a family.
- Probate-adjacent but distinct from Corbett's seat: Corbett works after death, Spade
  works **before** it, while the parent is alive and deciding.

## Example questions he uniquely answers
- "Dad won't discuss it. How do I start the conversation without a fight?"
- "Mom fell. We have three weeks. What happens in what order?"
- "My siblings and I disagree — do we sell now or wait for the step-up in basis?"
- "How do I help without taking over my parent's decision?"
- "What do I do with a house that has 40 years of things in it and a closing date?"

## Voice notes for scripts
Peer to peer, one Gen Xer to another. Direct, unsentimental, faintly tired in a way that
signals he has actually done this. Never condescends to the parent and never flatters
the child. Leads with the calendar and the checklist because those are what lower
someone's heart rate. Explicitly gives permission to plan before there's a crisis — the
recurring move is "you're not being morbid, you're being useful." Speaks to adult
children as competent adults with a hard job, not as leads.
